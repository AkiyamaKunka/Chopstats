Chopstats is a bring-your-own-key calorie tracker. It has no accounts and
no backend of ours. The user pastes an API key for an AI provider they
already pay for (Google Gemini, OpenAI, Anthropic, xAI, OpenRouter,
DeepSeek, Alibaba Qwen, ByteDance Doubao, Zhipu GLM); that provider
receives each photo being analyzed (with the user's dietary profile and a
short summary of the day's meals) and returns a nutrition estimate. Before
anything is sent to a provider for the first time, the app asks for
permission in a dialog that names the company and what is sent; Settings ›
"Stop Sharing with …" withdraws it. Meal text the user types goes to the
same provider; everything else stays on the phone in a local database,
apart from iCloud Backup and the restaurant lookup (below).

To test: a fresh install opens on Settings. Tap AI Provider › API Key;
Google Gemini is already selected; paste the key below into "Gemini
API key". Go to Today › "Log" › "From recent photos", allow Photos
access, pick a food photo and tap Allow in the consent dialog; the
estimate appears after a few seconds. Without food photos on the test
device, use "Log" › "Describe a meal" (e.g. "a bowl of beef noodle
soup"), or share a food image from Safari or Photos into Chopstats via
the share sheet. There is no in-app camera: photos come from the system
Camera app.

Settings › Watch Camera Roll (optional) shows the same consent with a
notice, needs full Photos access (limited access only shows a hint),
and as soon as it is on it sends the photos from the last 2 days, then
each new photo, to the selected provider; it uses Background App
Refresh to check for new photos while the app is closed.

AI Provider › Your own server is for people who run their own server
(the open-source server in the same repository) with an AI plan signed in
on it; it cannot be tested without such a server, and choosing an AI
there switches the app away from the Gemini key. Nothing is sold in the
app: there are no in-app purchases, and this option unlocks nothing. The
bitewise://setup link fills in that server's address and key after a
confirmation dialog.

From 1.6.0: "Log" › "My usual foods" logs a frequent meal again, with no
AI and nothing sent. Restaurant nutrition (Settings, off by default) needs
an Anthropic or OpenRouter key or the user's own server, so it is grayed
out with the demo key; a lookup sends chain and food names with amounts,
never the photo or its location, and "Use location" makes one Apple MapKit
query from the phone, with no location permission. "Meta glasses photos":
the app does not connect to glasses; a glasses photo with no food found on
the phone is not sent.

New in 1.8.0

iCloud Backup (Settings › Your Data, on by default) copies meals, weight,
measurements, workouts and meal pictures to the user's own iCloud Drive.
We run no server and cannot read it; API keys and settings are never in
it. The app does not use HealthKit. Signed in to iCloud, launch shows
"iCloud Backup is on" (Keep On / Turn Off) and nothing uploads before an
answer. To see it: log a meal, tap "Back Up Now" (the first can take a
minute), open Files › iCloud Drive › Chopstats. A reinstall offers
"Restore your meals?" (Not Now skips it). Signed out, the line under the
switch asks to sign in to iCloud.

Correct with a note: tap a meal on Today; at the end of Edit meal, under
"Correct with a note", type e.g. "only half the rice" and tap "Correct
with AI" (works with the demo key, photo or manual meal). Sent: the note
plus that meal's text, foods, numbers and earlier notes; no photo. The
answer fills the form; nothing is saved until "Save"; notes stay with the
meal.

Photos taken before 4 a.m. also carry the day before's meals; older
installs are asked again first ("What Chopstats sends has changed").
